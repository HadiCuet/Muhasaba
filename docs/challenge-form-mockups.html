Challenge form — restructuring “What counts” + “Time”

Four takes on challenge_form_screen.dart. The first is today’s form, reproduced faithfully (including the end-date reset) so the others have something to beat. Toggle the mode and window controls inside each phone — they behave like the real thing.

Light theme only; colours are the real ColorScheme.fromSeed(0xFF2E7D5B) roles and the amber behind pair from theme.dart.

What’s actually wrong today

  1. Two boxes ask for the same number. In Days completed mode the target is a day count, so “40 nights Tahajjud” means typing 40 under WHAT COUNTS and picking 40 days under TIME — different headings, no explanation of the difference.
  2. Nothing catches the impossible case. Target 40 days inside a 30-day window saves fine, and the pace chip says “Behind · 2/day” — in days mode you can never log more than 1 a day.
  3. The headings don’t describe their contents. “What counts” holds how much; the actual “what” (title, category) is up in THE GOAL. “Time” means deadline, but the next group holds a reminder time.
  4. Target is a naked number. The Unit field sits below it, so you type “30” before saying 30 of what — and in days mode Unit disappears while the label still just says “Target”.
  5. “One tap logs” never says where the tap is, and the chips are bare numbers — 33 next to 100 reads as a typo unless you already think in tasbih.
  6. “Finish within” never shows the resulting end date — and checking it breaks it. Switch to “Between two dates” to see your deadline and _end is still null, so Ends falls back to the start date: your 30-day challenge silently becomes a 1-day one.

B · Minimal fix lowest risk

Same two groups, same widget tree, same _WindowKind enum. Renamed headings, unit moved above target, and in One per day mode the window is linked to the target by default — so the duplicate 40 only exists if you deliberately unlink it.

Fixes

  • “How much” / “By when” describe their contents; “time” no longer collides with reminder time.
  • Unit is chosen before the target, and the target carries it as a suffix (40 days, 30 juz).
  • Step chips read +1 juz and say where the tap happens; 33 is labelled tasbih.
  • Resolved date range always visible, in the form and the preview — kills the invisible deadline.
  • Unlink and set a target that can’t fit and you get the amber warning instead of a silent save.

Try it

  • Tap the green switch to unlink, then pick 30 days against a 40-day target.
  • Switch to Total amount — unit, step chips and the three-way window appear.
Cost: a day’s work. Mostly ARB churn — ~8 renamed keys across 23 files, plus a _linkWindowToTarget bool and a validator. No schema change, no migration.

C · One plan, in order recommended

“What counts” and “Time” merge into one group, and the shape question is lifted out in front as two explained cards. Everything after it is ordered by what it depends on, so the duplicate day-count is structurally impossible: a streak asks for its length once, then only how much slack to allow.

Why this one

  • The two 40s can’t both exist — “Spread over” is expressed relative to the target you just set (Exactly 40 days), not as an independent number.
  • The cards teach the distinction with examples, which the segmented labels never could at that width — and they’re the concept users actually have to grasp.
  • A total reads as amount + of what on one line, so a bare number is never asked for.
  • Fewer visible controls in each mode than today, despite doing more.

Try it

  • Spread over → A longer window — slack becomes explicit and the footer recomputes the pace.
  • Tap A total to reach — the whole plan section swaps.
Cost: 2–3 days. Real restructuring of _countsChildren/_timeChildren into one builder, and _WindowKind gains an exact case (or becomes a derived value). Edit mode has to map existing rows back onto the new shape — a 40/40 row must land on Exactly, a 40/60 row on Longer. Still no schema change.

🌍Sentence order is English-shaped. In Arabic and Urdu the verb, number and window all move — and this is the screen where a mistranslation silently changes the maths.

D · Sentence builder delightful, risky

One editable sentence; tap any underlined slot to change it. Everything secondary drops into a Details group. The relationship between the numbers is unambiguous because grammar carries it — you literally can’t read “40 nights within 40 days” as two unrelated fields.

Strengths

  • Best comprehension of the four. The user reads back exactly what they’re committing to.
  • Shortest screen — one scroll, no group hunting.

Why I’d hesitate

  • 23 languages. A sentence with 4 embedded interactive slots is not an ARB string — it’s a word-order-dependent widget layout. ar/ur/fa/ps invert it; Turkish and Uzbek push the verb to the end.
  • Arabic plurals are dual/few/many, so “nights” has to agree with a number the translator can’t see.
  • Accessibility: inline tappable slots inside a wrapping paragraph are awkward for screen readers and for large text sizes, where the sentence reflows into something hard to parse.
  • Validation has nowhere natural to live — no field to attach an error to.
Cost: a week, and an ongoing translation burden every time the sentence changes. Worth prototyping only if you’d accept shipping it English-first.

My read: C is the right structure and B is the right amount of work. They’re not exclusive — B’s fixes (unit-before-target, labelled step chips, always-visible date range, the impossible-target warning, and the _end fallback bug) are all inside C too. If you want to ship in one sitting, do B and keep C for when you next touch the screen. If you want to only touch this screen once, go straight to C.

The _end ?? _startOrToday collapse in variant A is a real bug worth fixing on its own branch regardless of which structure wins.
